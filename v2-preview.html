Why this matters

Storm Bert changed the conversation in Bloxham.

Between 22 and 24 November 2024, intense rainfall caused serious flooding across the village. Community information indicates that around 65 properties were affected, although a precise total is not known. At least five vehicles were also badly affected.

Blocked drains and gullies made matters worse, but drainage maintenance alone cannot solve the problem. Large volumes of water also run rapidly off the higher ground surrounding Bloxham.

The figure of around 65 affected properties is a community estimate and is broader than the number formally recorded in official reporting.

The Bloxham approach

Two parts of one solution.

Reduce how much water arrives at the same time, while making sure the village can drain as effectively as possible.

1

Inside the village

Keep the drainage system working

Drains, gullies, culverts and channels need to be inspected, cleared and maintained so rainfall falling on Bloxham itself can get away as efficiently as possible.

Maintain capacity
2

Around the village

Slow the flow from the hills

NFM can temporarily hold water upstream using field bunds, leaky woody dams, ponds, swales, wetlands and land-management changes. The objective is to delay the peak.

A nearby example

The Evenlode shows NFM working.

The neighbouring Evenlode Catchment Partnership has delivered NFM in rural Oxfordshire for several years. On Littlestock Brook above Milton-under-Wychwood, measures include field storage bunds and leaky woody dams.

During Storm Bert, a field bund above Milton-under-Wychwood held back about 1,700 m³ of floodwater. The partnership reported that houses along Littlestock Brook did not flood on that occasion.
