Why this matters

Flooding is not new to Bloxham.

Storm Bert in November 2024 was the latest and most severe episode in recent community experience. Community information indicates that around 65 properties were affected, although a precise total is not known, and at least five vehicles were badly affected.

Historical evidence indicates that Bloxham has been wrestling with flooding, water and drainage for more than 150 years. The past can therefore form part of the evidence base for what we do next.

The figure of around 65 affected properties is a community estimate and is broader than the number formally recorded in official reporting.

The Bloxham approach

Two areas. One objective.

Rain falling ON Bloxham needs to drain efficiently. Rain falling OUTSIDE the village in the wider catchment needs, where practical, to be slowed and temporarily stored.

Area 1

Rain falling ON Bloxham

Drain it efficiently.

  • Keep drains and gullies clear
  • Maintain drainage capacity
  • Identify restrictions and pinch points
  • Allow rainfall on the village to get away
↓ ↓ ↓
BLOXHAM

Give the village drainage system time and capacity to cope.

← delay the peak →

Area 2

Rain falling OUTSIDE Bloxham

Slow the flow.

  • Leaky woody dams
  • Field bunds and swales
  • Ponds, wetlands and temporary storage
  • Release water more gradually after the storm peak

Historical documents archive

150+ years of flooding: learning from Bloxham's past.

Old maps, drainage records, wells, pumps and newspaper reports can help show how water moved through Bloxham, what previous generations proposed and which problems have persisted.

Historic maps

Village maps, old watercourses, drainage routes and changes in development. Material will be added as it is obtained.

Newspaper archive

Contemporary reporting showing that debates about flooding, drainage, responsibility and cost were taking place generations ago.

The question behind the archive

What did Bloxham know then that could help Bloxham now?

The archive will look carefully for recurring locations, historic drainage routes, previous interventions and proposals — while distinguishing documentary evidence from modern interpretation.
