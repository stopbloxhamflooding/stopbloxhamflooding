Funding to help
slow and store water.

There can be financial support for measures that reduce runoff, temporarily store water and improve flood and drought resilience. This page gives a practical first look at some of the current schemes before linking to the full government guidance.

Important

These examples are a starting point, not funding advice. Eligibility depends on the farm, land, location and proposed work. Rates and schemes can change, so always check the linked GOV.UK guidance before making decisions.

Why it matters

Could winter water become an asset?

Temporary NFM storage is designed to hold stormwater and release it gradually. In suitable places, separate or complementary farm water-storage measures may also help retain winter rainfall for use during dry periods.

For Bloxham, the opportunity is to explore measures that can reduce the peak flow reaching the village while remaining practical for the farm. In some circumstances, managing winter rainfall more effectively may also strengthen farm resilience during dry summers.

Not every option will suit every holding. Some measures are temporary flood storage; others may involve permanent ponds, reservoirs or different water-management arrangements. Those can carry different planning, abstraction, licensing and funding requirements.

Current examples

What support might be available?

The figures below are from current 2026 government guidance and are shown to help landowners understand the scale of support before following the official links.

Capital Grants 2026

Improved Natural Flood Management

Up to £25,000 is available within the Natural Flood Management capital group, subject to eligibility and the rules of the scheme.

  1. Small leaky woody dam — £461.39 each
  2. Large leaky woody dam — £764.42 each
  3. Create or restore pond up to 2ha — £1,879.46 per pond
  4. Earth banks / soil bunds — £195.61 per unit
  5. Swales — £7.52 per m²
  6. Check dams — £85.29 each

These payments are for eligible capital items. The detailed rules, specifications and evidence requirements matter.

Countryside Stewardship Higher Tier

Flood and drought resilience

Higher Tier can provide annual payments for qualifying land-management actions over a number of years.

  1. Flood mitigation on permanent grassland — £330/ha/year
  2. Flood mitigation on arable reversion to grassland — £740/ha/year
  3. Manage arable features for flood/drought resilience — £1,241/ha/year
  4. Manage grassland for flood/drought resilience — £938/ha/year
  5. Enhanced floodplain storage supplement — £366/ha/year

Availability depends on the land, the base action and Higher Tier agreement requirements.

Other relevant Higher Tier actions

Waterbodies, rivers and wetlands

Depending on the site, other actions may be relevant where river, floodplain, pond or wetland restoration forms part of a wider scheme.

  1. Make room for the river to move — £1,489/ha
  2. Connect river and floodplain habitats — £1,242/ha
  3. Manage riparian / water-edge habitat — £1,186/ha
  4. Manage ponds up to 2ha — £424 per pond
  5. Create fen, reedbed or wetland mosaics — £1,605/ha

These actions have specific habitat, location and management requirements and will not be suitable for every farm.

A practical starting point

Interested in exploring an idea?

The aim is to make it easier for local landowners to investigate opportunities without committing to a scheme at the outset.

1. Identify the water problem

Where does runoff form, where does it concentrate, and where might it be possible to slow or temporarily store it?

2. Check the farm impact

Consider access, cropping, grazing, drainage, maintenance and whether the proposal complements the farm business.

3. Match the funding

Use professional advice to identify the scheme, capital item or land-management action that best fits the proposed measure.

4. Develop it collaboratively

Work with the landowner, technical advisers and relevant authorities so the proposal is practical, funded and does not simply transfer flood risk elsewhere.
